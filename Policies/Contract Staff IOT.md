# PrimeCore Minerals  
## Contractor BYOD and On-Site Network Access Policy

### 1. Purpose

This policy sets the conditions under which contracted staff may use their own devices while working on-site for PrimeCore Minerals and access approved PrimeCore network services.

Contractors are given limited access only to the systems, files, and services required to complete their contracted work. Access is temporary, role-based, and limited to the approved contract period.

### 2. Scope

This policy applies to all contractors, consultants, subcontractors, and labour-hire staff who use personally owned laptops, mobile phones, or tablets while on a PrimeCore site or while accessing PrimeCore systems.

Network and system access must automatically expire at the approved end date. Any extension must be approved by the contractor’s PrimeCore supervisor and recorded by IT before access is continued.

### 4. Approved Access

Contractors may access only:

- The designated contractor or guest network.
- Their assigned PrimeCore account and approved collaboration services.
- Files, folders, project spaces, and applications specifically shared for their contracted role.
- Approved internet access and services required to complete their work.

Access must follow the principle of least privilege. Contractors must not be granted broader access merely because it may be convenient.

### 5. Network Restrictions

Personal contractor devices must connect only to the designated contractor or guest network. They must not be connected directly to PrimeCore’s internal employee, server, management, or operational network segments.

Contractors must not access or attempt to access:

- Internal network drives, servers, databases, or systems not specifically assigned to them.
- Network-management systems, monitoring tools, firewall controls, or administrative systems.
- Other employees’ accounts, emails, files, devices, or shared workspaces.
- PrimeCore printers, scanners, storage devices, or other corporate equipment without written approval.
- Internal corporate resources through unauthorised remote-access tools, VPNs, file-sharing services, or personal hotspots.

### 6. Device Security Requirements

Before a personal device is allowed to access PrimeCore services, the contractor must ensure that it:

- Uses a supported and updated operating system.
- Has a password, PIN, or biometric screen lock enabled.
- Has current security updates and anti-malware protection where supported.
- Is not rooted, jailbroken, or deliberately modified to bypass security controls.
- Is used only by the authorised contractor when accessing PrimeCore information.
- Does not contain known malware, unauthorised tools, or insecure software that presents a risk to PrimeCore.

PrimeCore may deny or remove access if a device does not meet these requirements.

### 7. Information Handling

Contractors may view, edit, and share only information that has been provided for their approved work.

Contractors must not:

- Access, search for, or attempt to open files that have not been shared with them.
- Copy PrimeCore information to personal email, cloud storage, USB drives, or unapproved applications.
- Print PrimeCore information using personal or PrimeCore printers without approval.
- Share PrimeCore information with unauthorised people, including other contractors.
- Retain PrimeCore files after their contract access has ended.

If a contractor receives access to information they believe they should not see, they must stop viewing it and report it immediately to their PrimeCore supervisor or IT. They must not copy, download, print, share, or alter the information.

### 8. Acceptable Use

Contractor access is provided for approved work only. Reasonable personal use of the contractor network may be permitted, but it must not interfere with work, consume excessive data, create security risks, or breach PrimeCore policies.

Contractors must not use PrimeCore network access for illegal downloads, copyright infringement, adult content, harassment, bullying, discriminatory content, malicious activity, excessive streaming, gaming, cryptocurrency mining, or any activity that is illegal or inconsistent with a respectful workplace.

### 9. Monitoring and Privacy

Contractors should not expect their use of PrimeCore networks, accounts, and services to be private. PrimeCore may monitor, log, filter, audit, or investigate access where reasonably required for security, operational management, legal compliance, incident response, or policy enforcement.

PrimeCore does not take ownership of a contractor’s personal device. However, it may remove PrimeCore accounts, permissions, sessions, and work data from the device when access ends or a security risk is identified.

### 10. Reporting Security Issues

Contractors must immediately report:

- Lost or stolen devices that have accessed PrimeCore systems.
- Suspicious emails, links, attachments, or login prompts.
- Suspected malware or unusual device behaviour.
- Accidental access to unauthorised files or systems.
- Any suspected disclosure, loss, or misuse of PrimeCore information.

Reports must be made to the contractor’s PrimeCore supervisor or the IT/security contact.

### 11. Contract End and Access Removal

At the approved contract end date, PrimeCore IT must:

- Disable the contractor’s PrimeCore account and network access.
- Remove access to shared files, project spaces, applications, remote access, and collaboration tools.
- Revoke active sessions, passwords, access tokens, certificates, and multi-factor authentication methods.
- Confirm that PrimeCore information has been returned, transferred, or removed as required.
- Record completion of offboarding.

If a contractor’s engagement ends early, their supervisor must notify IT immediately so access can be removed without delay.

### 12. Breaches

PrimeCore may immediately suspend or remove contractor access where a breach of this policy, a security risk, or unauthorised activity is identified.

Serious or repeated breaches may result in termination of access, termination of the contract, recovery of losses where appropriate, and reporting to relevant authorities.
